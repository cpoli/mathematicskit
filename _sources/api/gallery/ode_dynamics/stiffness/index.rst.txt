Stiffness and boundary-value problems
-------------------------------------

Why explicit integrators stall on stiff systems: Dahlquist's A-stability,
Curtiss and Hirschfelder's BDF, Robertson's stiff kinetics, Radau IIA
implicit Runge-Kutta, and boundary-value problems by collocation.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Applied to the test equation y&#x27; = \lambda y, a one-step method multiplies y by a stability function R(z), with z = \lambda h. Dahlquist (1963) called a method A-stable if |R(z)| \le 1 on the whole left half-plane, so that no decaying mode can ever be amplified, whatever the step size. Explicit methods never qualify, since their R is a polynomial and grows without bound. RK4&#x27;s region reaches only z \approx -2.785 on the real axis. Backward Euler&#x27;s R(z) = 1/(1 - z) is A-stable. Dahlquist also proved that no A-stable linear multistep method can exceed order 2 (his &quot;second barrier&quot;).">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="A two-point boundary-value problem fixes conditions at both ends, so there is no initial state to march forward from. Collocation solves for the whole solution at once. It takes a piecewise polynomial on a mesh and requires it to satisfy the ODE exactly at a few collocation points per interval and to meet the boundary conditions, which gives one nonlinear system for Newton&#x27;s method. De Boor and Swartz (1973) proved that collocation with k Gauss points per interval converges with order 2k at the mesh nodes (&quot;superconvergence&quot;). This made it the basis of BVP codes such as COLSYS and scipy.integrate.solve_bvp, which uses the closely related 3-point Lobatto IIIA scheme.">
