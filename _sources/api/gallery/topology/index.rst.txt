Examples
========

This gallery walks through every public feature of ``mathematicskit.topology``:
simplicial complexes and their homology, the fundamental group, the
classification of surfaces, fixed-point theorems, invariants of curves,
and the topological analysis of point clouds.

Each script in this gallery is self-contained and can be run directly with
``python examples/topology/<section>/<script>.py``.

Sections
--------

- **curves** -- Gauss's linking number and Hopf's turning number.
- **surfaces** -- the Möbius strip, the Klein bottle, the classification
  of surfaces, and critical points of height functions.
- **homology** -- the Smith normal form, Betti numbers, Poincaré's
  homology and the Euler-Poincaré formula, and the fundamental group.
- **complexes** -- barycentric subdivision, products and the Künneth
  formula, and the Mayer-Vietoris sequence.
- **fixed_points** -- the Poincaré-Hopf index, Brouwer's fixed-point
  theorem, the Lefschetz number, and Sperner's lemma.
- **data** -- Vietoris-Rips and Čech complexes, persistent homology, its
  stability, and Mapper.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Is homology a property of the space or of the chosen triangulation? Brouwer&#x27;s simplicial approximation (1911) answers it. Any continuous map is close to a simplicial one after subdividing finely enough. Alexander (1915) used this to prove that homology is a topological invariant. Barycentric subdivision replaces every simplex by the chains of its faces. The simplex counts grow about sixfold per round for surfaces, while the Betti numbers do not change.">

Today this is the Vietoris-Rips complex: at scale r (balls of radius r), a simplex for every set of points at most 2r apart. Sampled from a figure eight, the complex at small scales is dust. Fixed points
------------

Vector-field indices, Brouwer, Lefschetz, and Sperner.


.. raw:: html

  <div id='sg-tag-list' class='sphx-glr-tag-list'></div>


.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbnails">

.. thumbnail-parent-div-open

.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Walk around a small circle enclosing a zero of a planar vector field, and the field&#x27;s direction turns a whole number of times. That number is the zero&#x27;s index: +1 for sources, sinks and centres, -1 for saddles. Poincaré (1885) proved that on a closed surface the indices sum to the Euler characteristic, and Hopf (1927) extended this to all dimensions. For a disc, with the field pointing outward on the boundary, the sum is \chi(\text{disc}) = 1. Below, a field with two sources and a saddle gives 1 + 1 - 1 = 1.">

.. only:: html

  .. image:: /api/gallery/topology/fixed_points/images/thumb/sphx_glr_plot_01_poincare_hopf_index_thumb.png
    :alt:

  :doc:`/api/gallery/topology/fixed_points/plot_01_poincare_hopf_index`

.. raw:: html

      <div class="sphx-glr-thumbnail-title">The Poincaré-Hopf index theorem (1885-1927)</div>
    </div>


.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Every continuous map of a disc (or triangle) to itself has a fixed point. The proof here is constructive. Label each vertex of a fine triangulation with a coordinate the map does not increase. That labelling obeys Sperner&#x27;s rule, so some small triangle carries all three labels, and on it the map nearly fixes every coordinate. Refining the grid closes in on a fixed point, which matches a Newton-type solve with scipy.optimize.fsolve.">

.. only:: html

  .. image:: /api/gallery/topology/fixed_points/images/thumb/sphx_glr_plot_02_brouwer_fixed_point_thumb.png
    :alt:

  :doc:`/api/gallery/topology/fixed_points/plot_02_brouwer_fixed_point`

.. raw:: html

      <div class="sphx-glr-thumbnail-title">Brouwer's fixed-point theorem (1911)</div>
    </div>


.. raw:: html

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Solomon Lefschetz assigned to every self-map f of a complex the number L(f) = \sum_k (-1)^k \operatorname{tr}(f_*: H_k \to H_k), and proved that L(f) \ne 0 forces a fixed point. Hopf showed the same alternating trace can be computed on chains, where it counts the simplices f maps to themselves. The identity has L = \chi. A rotation of the circle has L = 0 and indeed moves every point. A reflection has L = 2: its two fixed points. The antipodal map of the sphere has L = 1 + (-1)^3 = 0. The converse fails: L = 0 does not rule fixed points out, as the identity of the torus shows.">

  .. image:: /api/gallery/topology/fixed_points/images/thumb/sphx_glr_plot_03_lefschetz_fixed_point_thumb.png

    </div>


.. raw:: html

Emanuel Sperner proved that an odd number of small triangles carry all three labels. Picture labels 0-1 edges as doors: one enters from the bottom side, and following doors from room to room can only stop in a fully labelled room.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="H. J. S. Smith showed that integer row and column operations bring any integer matrix to a diagonal D = UAV with d_1 \mid d_2 \mid \cdots.     <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Glue the ends of a strip with a half twist and the surface has only one side. In triangle terms, its triangles cannot all be oriented so that neighbours traverse their shared edge in opposite directions. Propagating orientations around the strip comes back reversed. The untwisted band (an annulus) has no such obstruction. Both have \chi = 0, so orientability is what tells them apart, along with the number of boundary circles: one for the Möbius strip, two for the annulus.">
